Title: Three Cups (ሶስት ሲኒ)
Language: Amharic

Storyteller: a grandmother, a stylised character, not a real person. She speaks to her grandchild.
Voice: stock TTS voice, warm, slow, older woman. No real face or voice is used.

What the ceremony is:
- Buna (coffee) is made and served at home, often every day, for family and neighbours.
- Fresh cut grass (ketema) is spread on the floor. Frankincense (etan) burns in a small clay burner.
- The green beans are washed, then roasted in a flat pan over charcoal. They turn brown, then dark, and crack.
- The host carries the hot pan around the room so everyone can smell the smoke. Guests wave it toward themselves.
- The beans are pounded by hand in a wooden mortar (mukecha) with a metal pestle (zenezena).
- The ground coffee goes into a clay pot with a long neck (jebena) and boils on the coals.
- Small cups without handles (sini) stand on a wooden tray (rekebot).
- Popcorn (fendisha) or roasted barley (kolo) is served with the coffee.
- There are three rounds from the same grounds: abol (first, strongest), tona (second), bereka (third, the blessing).
- People talk during the ceremony: news, problems, advice. It can take an hour or more. Leaving before the third cup is
  rude, because the third cup carries the blessing.

What she wants the grandchild to keep:
- The ceremony is not about the coffee. It is about time given to each other.
- Anyone who passes the door is welcome to sit.
- One day the grandchild will roast the beans for her own family.

Shape:
- Open on hands and smoke, close on the third cup being handed to the grandchild.
- Night or late afternoon light from a doorway, charcoal glow, warm and dark.
- Style: painterly, warm, not photoreal. Same grandmother in every shot she appears in.
